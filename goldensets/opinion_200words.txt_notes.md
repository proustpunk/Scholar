**Layer 2: Per-Concept Breakdown**

**Whole Food Philosophy**
This concept prioritizes foods in their natural state, such as vegetables and fruits, while explicitly rejecting processed ingredients. Processed items are criticized for containing excessive sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats that lead to inflammation and chronic disease. **Unlike specific dietary labels, this approach focuses on ingredient quality rather than macronutrient ratios.**

**Specific Dietary Models**
The text surveys several established frameworks, including the Mediterranean diet, plant-based diets, Keto, and intermittent fasting. It characterizes the Mediterranean diet as widely considered the healthiest, Keto as unsustainable long-term, and plant-based options as environmentally superior. **This section contrasts the sustainability and health outcomes of different structured eating plans against the general whole food baseline.**

**Individualization vs. Universal Rules**
This thread argues that no single diet works for every person, emphasizing that individual physiological responses vary. It promotes behavioral strategies like mindful eating, cooking at home, and moderation over rigid adherence to one specific plan. **Contradicting the idea of a "best" universal diet, this concept asserts that personal adaptation is the ultimate metric for success.**

**Layer 3: The Synthesis Crib Sheet** (technical accuracy + integration)

**Key Definitions & Evaluations**
*   **Whole Foods:** Provide essential nutrients and fiber; oppose processed foods causing inflammation.
*   **Mediterranean Diet:** Highlighted as "widely considered the healthiest"; includes olive oil, fish, veggies, limited red meat.
*   **Keto:** Effective short-term but deemed "unsustainable."
*   **Plant-Based:** Cited specifically for environmental benefits.
*   **Cholesterol/Eggs:** Debunked past concerns; eggs are considered nutritious.

**Concept Integration Logic**
1.  **Foundation:** Start with whole foods/avoid processed ingredients (universal baseline).
2.  **Selection:** Choose a model like Mediterranean (highly recommended) or Keto (short-term only).
3.  **Adjustment:** Modify based on individual body response and sustainability.

**Comparison of Conflicting Views**
*   **Universal Solution vs. Personal Fit:** The text initially suggests the Mediterranean diet is the "healthiest" (implying a universal standard) but concludes there is "no perfect diet for all people" (rejecting universality).
*   **Resolution:** The text resolves this by prioritizing "listening to your body" and "moderation" over strict model adherence.

**"Gotcha" Exam Points**
*   **Keto Sustainability:** A professor may ask why Keto is discouraged for long-term use; the text explicitly calls it "unsustainable," despite short-term weight control benefits.
*   **Secondary Benefits:** Do not confuse health outcomes with environmental ones; the text links plant-based diets specifically to the *environment*, while linking Mediterranean diets to *health*.
