Procedure for conducting a titration experiment:

1. Prepare equipment: burette, pipette, Erlenmeyer flask, white tile, clamp stand, wash bottle.
2. Rinse burette with distilled water, then with the titrant solution (NaOH).
3. Fill burette with titrant. Record initial volume to 0.05 mL precision.
4. Use a pipette to transfer 25.00 mL of analyte (HCl) to the Erlenmeyer flask.
5. Add 2-3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator to the flask. Solution is colorless.
6. Place flask on white tile under burette. Slowly add titrant while swirling continuously.
7. Watch for first permanent pink color that persists for 30 seconds. This is the endpoint.
8. Record final burette volume. Calculate volume of titrant used.
9. Repeat titration at least 3 times. Discard results if volumes differ by more than 0.10 mL.
10. Calculate average titrant volume from concordant results.

Data collection:
- Initial burette reading: 0.25 mL
- Final burette reading: 18.75 mL
- Volume used: 18.50 mL
- Run 2: 18.45 mL
- Run 3: 18.55 mL
- Average: 18.50 mL

Calculations:
Number of moles NaOH = Molarity × Volume (L)
For NaOH with concentration 0.100 M:
Moles NaOH = 0.100 × 0.01850 = 0.00185 mol

Stoichiometry: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (1:1 mole ratio)
Moles HCl = Moles NaOH = 0.00185 mol
Molarity HCl = Moles / Volume (L)
Molarity HCl = 0.00185 / 0.02500 = 0.0740 M

Precautions:
- Remove air bubbles from burette tip before starting.
- Titrate slowly near endpoint. Add titrant dropwise.
- Swirl constantly to ensure thorough mixing.
- Read meniscus at eye level to avoid parallax error.
- Use fresh indicator solution. Phenolphthalein degrades over time.
- Clean all glassware thoroughly. Contamination ruins results.
- Use white tile to observe color change more clearly.
- Record all measurements to proper significant figures.
- Dispose of chemicals according to safety guidelines.
- Wear safety goggles and lab coat at all times.
