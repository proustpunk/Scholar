The Roman Empire began its decline in the 3rd century AD. Political instability plagued the empire with 26 emperors in 50 years. Economic problems arose from debased currency and inflation. Barbarian invasions pressed on the frontiers. The empire split into eastern and western halves in 285 AD. The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when Romulus Augustulus was deposed. The Eastern Roman Empire continued for another thousand years as Byzantium. Many factors contributed: military overspending, corruption, slave labor, natural disasters, and lead poisoning from water pipes. Christianity's rise changed Roman values and priorities. The Huns pushed Germanic tribes into Roman territory. The Visigoths sacked Rome in 410 AD. The Vandals sacked Rome again in 455 AD. Each sack weakened the empire's prestige and power. The western provinces gradually became independent kingdoms. Roman infrastructure—roads, aqueducts, public buildings—fell into disrepair. Literacy and trade declined. The Dark Ages followed.
