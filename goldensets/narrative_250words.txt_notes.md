**Layer 1: The Sticky Analogy**

Imagine a gigantic, luxurious house that has been standing for centuries. The foundation is cracking because the owners stopped paying for maintenance and started spending lavishly on parties. The plumbing leaks lead poisoning, and the walls are thin enough to hear neighbors planning a break-in.

One day, the house splits down the middle; the east wing remains sturdy, but the west wing is left unguarded and underfunded. Raiders storm into the west wing, looting valuables and breaking windows, while the residents inside argue over who gets the last slice of pizza.

Eventually, the west wing’s roof caves in, not from one single blow, but from years of neglect, internal fighting, and external pressure. **The core lesson is that collapse is rarely caused by a single event, but by a compounding series of structural failures and external pressures that erode resilience over time.**

**Layer 2: The Translation**

The fall of the Western Roman Empire was a multifactorial process involving political, economic, and military degradation. Legitimacy eroded as political instability led to twenty-six emperors in fifty years, preventing consistent governance or long-term planning.

Economically, the state relied on debased currency, which triggered inflation and distorted trade incentives. Militarily, the empire faced the dual pressure of internal overspending and external invasions by groups like the Huns, Visigoths, and Vandals.

The division in 285 AD isolated the West from the stronger Eastern economy, leaving it particularly vulnerable. **The primary tension was between the empire's vast military obligations and its shrinking economic capacity to support them, exacerbated by internal corruption and shifting cultural values.**

**Layer 3: The Exam Crib Sheet**

**Key Dates & Events:**
*   **285 AD:** Empire splits into Eastern and Western halves.
*   **410 AD:** Visigoths sack Rome.
*   **455 AD:** Vandals sack Rome.
*   **476 AD:** Western Roman Empire falls (deposition of Romulus Augustulus).

**Contributing Factors:**
*   **Political:** Instability (26 emperors in 50 years).
*   **Economic:** Debased currency, inflation, decline in trade and literacy.
*   **Social/Military:** Military overspending, corruption, slave labor dependency, rise of Christianity changing values.
*   **Environmental/Infrastructure:** Lead poisoning, natural disasters, disrepair of roads/aqueducts.

**Application Scenarios:**
1.  **Economic Collapse:** Analyzing how currency debasement led to inflation, reducing the state's ability to pay armies.
2.  **Geopolitical Fragmentation:** Examining why the split in 285 AD left the Western Empire defenseless against Germanic tribes pushed by the Huns.

**Gotcha Points:**
*   The Eastern Empire (Byzantium) continued for another thousand years; the fall specifically refers to the *Western* half.
*   Multiple sacks (410 AD and 455 AD) are cited as events that cumulatively weakened prestige and power, rather than a single instant cause.
